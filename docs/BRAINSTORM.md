# Pileup — Cards, Perks & Jokers Brainstorm

> A research-fueled brainstorm document. Drawn from study of Balatro Jokers, Super Auto Pets abilities, Hearthstone Battlegrounds hero powers, and Topdeck Automat mechanics. None of this is locked — it's raw material to react to, cut from, and combine.

---

## How to read this doc

Everything is split into the categories from the design doc:

1. **Card-specific abilities** (attached to one card in your pile)
   - 1A. Number shifts
   - 1B. Pair synergies
   - 1C. Comeback mechanics
   - 1D. Probability weighting
   - 1E. Other card-specific (utility, economy, conditional)

2. **Global Joker abilities** (warp the whole run)
   - 2A. Parity Jokers (odd/even)
   - 2B. Tie/draw Jokers
   - 2C. Probability Jokers
   - 2D. Comeback / underdog Jokers
   - 2E. Economy Jokers
   - 2F. Scaling Jokers (get stronger as run goes)
   - 2G. Battle-rule Jokers (warp the rules of combat itself)
   - 2H. Build-around Jokers (force a specific archetype)
   - 2I. Wildcard / chaos Jokers
   - 2J. Legendary / Rare Jokers

Each entry has a **rough rarity tag** (Common / Uncommon / Rare / Legendary) — Balatro-style. Common = early-shop fodder, Legendary = once-per-run game-changer.

## Card-mechanic ideas (foundational)

Things that aren't abilities exactly — they're properties cards can have. Think Balatro's *editions* (foil, holographic, polychrome) — they layer on top of any ability.

- **Foil card** — +20% flip chance.
- **Holo card** — On reveal, draws a 6th card to flip this battle.
- **Echo card** — Ability triggers twice.
- **Fragile card** — Cheaper in shop, but breaks (removed) after 3 battles.
- **Sealed card** — Can't be sold, but gains +1 value every 2 battles.
- **Cursed card** — Strong stats, but if it loses, you lose +1 HP.
- **Twin card** — Comes with a duplicate when bought (occupies 2 slots).
- **Mirror card** — Becomes a copy of opponent's first flipped card for that battle.
- **Negative card** — Doesn't take a slot in your pile (free slot card).

These are *modifiers*, not new cards. They're a different axis of upgrade — bought from a separate shop track or as battle rewards.

## Synergy combos worth designing for

The best card games have *named meta combos* the community discovers. Plan for these intentionally:

1. **The Anvil Build** — Sharpener + Whetstone + Buff Aura on a single high card. Brute force.
2. **The Engine** — Coin Press cards + Tycoon Joker + Compound Interest. Win through economy, not power.
3. **The Sniper** — Anchor + Sniper Build Joker + a value-10 card. The hammer always lands.
4. **The Pity Party** — Underdog cards + Bleed for Gold + Cornered Rat. Lose your way to victory.
5. **The Loop** — Mirror Match + Pair Hunter + Twin Stack Joker. Build around fishing for ties.
6. **The Slim** — Sell down to 6 cards + Slim Pile Joker + Fat Pile (oh wait that's contradictory — point being, players will try to break it).
7. **The Chaos Stack** — Coin Flip + Mystery Box + Wild Cards. Pure variance, peak entertainment.
8. **The Vampire** — Sacrifice card + Phoenix card + Rage Build. Each loss makes you stronger.

## Open questions for content design

- **How many cards in the base set?** Balatro has 150 Jokers. SAP has 166 pets. For Pileup, suggest aiming for 80–120 cards + 60–80 Jokers as launch content.
- **Drop rates for rarities?** Balatro uses 70/25/5 for Common/Uncommon/Rare; Legendary is locked behind specific spectrals. This shape works.
- **Tier-gating in shop tiers?** Confirmed in design doc — match it. Tier 1 of shop = mostly Common cards; Tier 5 = Rares + a chance at Legendary.
- **Opponent visibility?** Should you see the *Joker* your opponent picked before battle? (Battlegrounds shows it; SAP doesn't fully.) Knowing the rule warp helps you plan flips.
- **Joker stacking limits?** Balatro caps at 5. Battlegrounds is 1. Pileup's design doc says TBD — for content design, **2 slots** seems like the sweet spot: enough for a primary + supporting Joker, not so many that synergies get insane.

---

## Pacing notes

From genre research, the sweet spots are:

- **Shop turn:** 60–90 seconds for player decisions (Battlegrounds), or async unlimited (SAP).
- **Battle phase:** 20–40 seconds of auto-resolution. Pileup's 5 flips fits cleanly here.
- **Run length:** 8–12 battles, 25–35 minutes total.
- **Decisions per shop:** 3–5 meaningful actions per turn. Too few = boring; too many = analysis paralysis.

# VOLUME 2 — Wider Genre Research

> Round 2 of brainstorming, built on deeper research into Oaken Tower, The Bazaar, Backpack Battles, Dicey Dungeons, Luck Be a Landlord, and the wider PvP auto-battler scene. Each subsection starts with **what the game teaches** and ends with **ideas it sparks for Pileup**.

---

## Lessons from Oaken Tower

**What it is:** Synergy-heavy PvP auto-battler. You build a tower of items (not units) that fights another player's tower. Async, 92% Very Positive on Steam, **42-hour median playtime** at $7.99 — obsession-level retention. Direct competitor in spirit.

**Key mechanics worth stealing:**

- **Pre-run perk selection.** You pick **4 perks before the run starts** (out of 20+). One is randomly activated each run. This means each run has a *defined identity* from turn 1 — not "what Joker did I find?" but "what Joker did I plan around?" Massive design implication for Pileup.
- **Tags** (Holy, Moon, Frosty, Crystal, Toxic, Elven, Magic, etc.). Items have tags, and other items synergize *with tags*. Dagger Tome buffs daggers. Elven Orb buffs elven items. This is how you create archetype lanes.
- **Packs.** Items belong to "packs" you can enable/disable in setup. Lets players curate their RNG and removes archetypes they hate.
- **Multicast.** A stat that retriggers an item's ability multiple times. The most exciting scaling vector — players talk about "Multicast Gods" builds where a single item triggers 8 times.
- **Star ratings (★0/1/2/3).** Tripling an item upgrades it. Like Hearthstone Battlegrounds golden minions. Creates a meta-currency: "should I triple this or pivot?"
- **Cursed items.** Strong but with downsides. Adds risk-reward to shop.
- **NPC encounters between battles.** Pirate gives goblet + 10 gold. Warrior gives 2 random weapons. Adds variety beyond shop-then-battle.
- **"Fight the Oak"** — a target dummy mode for build testing. Players love it.

**The big warning:** Top community complaint is "only 3 viable builds; just go multihit and you win." Multicast scales so hard it eats the meta. **Lesson for Pileup:** if any one mechanic exponentially scales while others add linearly, that mechanic wins. Don't make probability weighting or any single ability path scale exponentially without a hard cap.

**Ideas this sparks for Pileup:**

- **Pre-run Joker selection.** Instead of finding all Jokers in shops, let players pick 2–4 Jokers *before the run* from their unlocked pool. One activates randomly when the run starts (like Oaken Tower's perks). This makes runs more identity-driven and reduces frustrating shop RNG.
- **Card tags.** Beyond just value (1–10), give cards tags: *Sword* (combat), *Coin* (econ), *Trick* (manipulation), *Spirit* (passive), *Ghost* (probability). Then design Jokers and abilities that synergize with tags. This adds a second axis to deckbuilding without complicating the base game.
- **Echo / Multicast equivalent.** A keyword that triggers a card's ability twice. Rare and contained. Could be a permanent buff, a Joker effect, or an item.
- **★ system for cards.** Triple a card to upgrade it (like Battlegrounds golden minions or Oaken Tower stars). Could be a way to thin a 10-card pile while keeping power: 3 copies of "5" become 1 mighty "5★" with stronger ability.
- **Cursed cards.** Strong cards with a downside. "10 of Skulls: value 10, but you take 1 HP if it loses." Risk-reward.
- **Pack/expansion toggles.** When you unlock more content, let players toggle which packs are in their shop pool. Power user feature, prevents new content from breaking old strategies.
- **"Fight the Oak"-equivalent practice mode.** Let players test builds against a static dummy pile. Helps theorycrafting; community will love it.

## Lessons from The Bazaar

**What it is:** Asynchronous PvP auto-battler from a former Hearthstone pro (Reynad). Ten-slot board. Items have cooldowns. Multi-hero design. Sandstorm sudden-death. Deeply considered design from a competitive-card-game veteran.

**Key mechanics worth stealing:**

- **Asynchronous matchmaking with ghost data.** You don't fight the *current* state of another player's board — you fight a *recorded snapshot* from when they were at the same day-count. This means:
  - No timers, infinite think time
  - You can quit mid-run and resume
  - Server keeps copies of boards at each day, matchmakes appropriately
  - **Confirmed perfect for Pileup's async mode in v1.**
- **5 rarity tiers** for items: Bronze, Silver, Gold, Diamond, Legendary. Same-tier item collisions in shop fuse into a higher-tier version. (Like Battlegrounds tripling, but only 2 needed.)
- **Days and Hours structure.** Each "day" has 6 "hours." Hour 3 = PvE encounter (varying difficulty). Hour 6 = PvP battle. Other hours = events, merchants, decisions. Adds *texture* to the run beyond "shop-battle-shop-battle."
- **Prestige / lives system.** Start with 20 prestige, lose some equal to your day-count when defeated. Goal: 10 PvP wins before prestige hits 0. Different from SAP's HP — lives count, not HP scales.
- **Sandstorm sudden death.** If a battle goes too long, increasing damage to both players forces a winner. Critical for stalling-build prevention.
- **Hero-locked card pools.** Each hero (Vanessa, Pygmalien, Dooley, Mak, etc.) has a 100-card exclusive pool. Lets each hero have a *fundamentally different game*. Some heroes are aggro, some scaling, some tempo.
- **Difficulty rating per hero.** New players steered toward easy heroes. Skill ceiling preserved for veterans. **Smart onboarding.**

**The big warning:** Predatory monetization soured the launch — they walked back "all cards earnable" promises and added paid card expansions, infuriating the community. Reynad's defensive responses made it worse. **Lesson for Pileup:** if you go free-to-play, lock yourself to cosmetic-only monetization in the design doc *and announcements*. Backtracking destroys trust.

**Ideas this sparks for Pileup:**

- **Async matchmaking via snapshots.** When a player finishes a shop turn, save their pile state as a ghost. Other players in async mode at the same battle-number get matched against random ghosts from that pool. **This is exactly the right v1 architecture.**
- **Day/hour run structure.** Instead of pure "shop → battle → shop → battle," add *texture*. Examples for Pileup:
  - Battle 1, 2: standard
  - Battle 3: PvE encounter (an NPC pile that's predictable, gives a guaranteed reward)
  - Battle 4: standard
  - Battle 5: shop with a special offer (more rerolls, free Joker)
  - Battle 6: standard
  - Battle 7: rest/decision event (heal HP, pick a free upgrade, etc.)
  - Battle 8: standard
  - Battle 9: boss-equivalent (PvE pile with a known nasty mechanic)
  - Battle 10: final
- **Sandstorm equivalent.** If neither player has won by flip 5 (e.g. 2-2 with one tie), the final flip's loser takes double HP damage. Forces decisive battles, prevents everyone hyper-defensive.
- **"Heroes" or "Captains" for Pileup.** Each captain has:
  - A unique starter pile (instead of everyone starting identical)
  - A small exclusive ability pool only they can buy
  - A passive bonus (Vanessa-equivalent: "Your value-10 cards: +1"; Dooley-equivalent: "Each pair triggers extra")
  - Difficulty rating for new players
  - Cosmetic identity for the lobby
  - This is a much bigger design decision — but worth flagging because it's how every successful long-tail PvP auto-battler differentiates.
- **Tier-fusion in shop.** When you buy a card you already own, they fuse into a +1 tier version. (Or: 2 copies fuse, simpler than Battlegrounds' 3.)

## Lessons from Backpack Battles

**What it is:** PvP auto-battler where your inventory is your team. Items have shapes (like Tetris), grid placement matters, adjacency creates synergies. 92% Very Positive, 14k+ reviews. *PocketGamer-coined "the bit everyone secretly enjoys anyway."*

**Key mechanics worth stealing:**

- **Adjacency synergies.** Items get bonuses based on what's next to them. Pan + Food = +1 damage per adjacent food. Encourages spatial thinking.
- **Stamina system.** Weapons consume stamina; over-stacking weapons starves them. Forces tradeoffs between attack and support.
- **Subclass at round 8.** Mid-run pivot point — pick 1 of 6 subclasses based on how your build is going. Lets players adapt to the RNG they got.
- **Sales and reservations.** Random items go on 50% sale. You can resell sale items at the same price (free try-out). You can reserve an item with a click — it stays in the shop next turn.
- **Crafting recipes.** Specific items combine into stronger items if placed correctly. Long-term planning: buy weak components knowing you'll merge them.
- **Fatigue mechanic.** If a battle drags too long, both players take increasing damage until someone dies. Same role as The Bazaar's sandstorm.
- **Class-specific item pools.** Ranger, Reaper, Berserker, Pyromancer, Mage, Puzzlebox — each has a fundamentally different starting backpack and item pool.

**Ideas this sparks for Pileup:**

- **Pile order matters (positional synergy).** Right now Pileup's pile is unordered — cards just shuffle. What if **the order of cards in your pile affects synergies**? Examples:
  - "Adjacent 5s buff each other"
  - "When this card is at position 1 in pile: +2 value"
  - "If your pile has 3 odd cards in a row: all your odd cards +1"
  - **Caveat:** This is a big design change. Risk: complicates UX for a game whose strength is simplicity. *Probably skip for v1*, but flag for v2.
- **Reserve a shop card for free.** Single click "freeze" mechanic — already in design doc, but Backpack Battles confirms it's a beloved feature. Worth keeping prominent.
- **Sale on shop items.** Random shop slots show up at 50% off, with full refund if you sell. Encourages experimentation with cards you wouldn't normally buy.
- **Crafting / fusion recipes.** Beyond simple tripling: specific card pairs combine. "Card A + Card B = Card C with combined ability." Gives players "aha!" discovery moments. Design challenge: can be opaque, may need an in-game recipe book.
- **Mid-run pivot point.** At battle 5 (mid-run), let players pick a "specialization" — a meta-perk that locks in their direction (e.g. Aggro / Econ / Comeback / Probability). This is the SAP/Backpack Battles subclass moment. Shapes the back half of the run.
- **Fatigue / sandstorm.** Already covered above. Both Bazaar and Backpack Battles use this — universally smart design.

## Lessons from Dicey Dungeons

**What it is:** Roguelike deckbuilder where you ARE a die. You roll dice each turn, slot them into equipment cards, and fight. Six characters, each with totally different mechanics. Beloved for accessibility + depth.

**Key mechanics worth stealing:**

- **Six characters with radically different rules.** Warrior plays straightforward damage. Inventor must DESTROY equipment after every fight to make new gadgets. Robot plays a push-your-luck blackjack mini-game to gain dice. Each one is essentially a different game. **This is Hearthstone Battlegrounds heroes turned up to 11.**
- **Episodes** (per character) introduce *modifier rules* to the run. Episode might be "every dice roll is a 1" or "enemies steal your equipment." Kept the game replayable for hundreds of hours.
- **Dice manipulation as core skill.** Re-roll, freeze, change face, split, combine. The whole game is "loaded dice management" — perfectly aligned with Pileup's probability-weighting axis.
- **Equipment slots with conditions.** Some equipment requires a die of a specific value, some any value, some matched values. Forces players to build around their character's dice pool.
- **Limited deck size, swap between battles.** Can't equip everything; must pick what's relevant for the next fight. Encourages adaptation.

**The genius observation:** Dicey Dungeons proved that **luck-based mechanics aren't a flaw — they're a feature when you give players tools to manipulate odds.** Reroll, freeze, change face, rig the game. *That's exactly what Pileup's probability-weighting category does.* You're on the right track.

**Ideas this sparks for Pileup:**

- **Modifier "challenges" as run variants.** After unlocking the main game, add weekly/seasonal modifier runs:
  - "All cards are odd"
  - "Pile size is 6 instead of 10"
  - "All flips are auto-revealed before the battle"
  - "First flip wins or loses the entire battle"
  - "You only get 1 Joker slot, but it's Legendary"
  - These are huge replayability multipliers and easy to author.
- **Cards that change values.** Borrow from dice manipulation:
  - "Reroll" — a one-shot item that re-randomizes a flipped card's value.
  - "Twist" — turns a value-X card into a value-(11-X) for that battle (e.g. 3 ↔ 8).
  - "Split" — turns one flipped card into two value-N/2 cards (rare/legendary).
  - "Lock" — pin a card's value at flip; can't be modified by opponent abilities.
- **Captain difficulty ratings.** Already mentioned for Bazaar; Dicey Dungeons reinforces it. Each Captain/Hero should have a 1–5 difficulty rating so new players don't pick the Inventor-equivalent.

## Lessons from Luck Be a Landlord

**What it is:** Roguelike where you spin a slot machine. Symbols you collect populate the reels; rare combos pay rent. **Perfectly captures "luck-based engine building."** Cited in Wikipedia as a co-namesake of the Balatro-genre.

**Key mechanics worth stealing:**

- **Symbol-on-reel synergies.** A Cherry pays 1. Cherry adjacent to Cherry pays 2 each. Three cherries lined up pay 5 each. **Adjacency + count multipliers.**
- **Rent / score-target loop.** Every X rounds you must hit a rent target. Miss it, you lose the run. Forces aggressive scaling and risk-taking.
- **Symbols destroy or transform other symbols.** A "Catfish" eats other fish symbols, gaining their power. A "Goldfish" produces gold. These chain reactions are the whole game.
- **Capsules** (rare reward boxes) give choices of rare symbols. Sweet drops of variance.
- **Removing symbols.** As critical as adding them. Bad symbols dilute your engine, so the shop has remove buttons.

**The genius observation:** Luck Be a Landlord proves that **a 100% randomized output (a slot spin) can be a deeply skill-based game IF the player controls the input pool.** That's exactly Pileup. The flip is random; the pile is the player's choice.

**Ideas this sparks for Pileup:**

- **Pile thinning is critical.** Already in design doc, but Luck Be a Landlord shows how *central* this feels. Make sure shop UX makes "remove a card" prominent and cheap. Players should feel like sculpting their pile, not just adding to it.
- **Cards that destroy other cards.** Rare, build-around effects:
  - "Cannibal" — when this flips, removes a random opponent card from their pile after this battle.
  - "Phoenix Down" — when this is sold, returns at +2 value.
  - "Black Hole" — destroys a card from your own pile, transferring its value to this card.
- **Score targets / "rent" battles.** Every 3rd battle is a "rent battle" — you must beat a minimum point threshold against a beefed-up PvE pile, or take heavy HP damage. Forces aggressive scaling and creates run pacing.
- **Capsule / chest rewards.** After a "rent battle" win, get a choice of 3 rare cards or a Joker. Same dopamine as Balatro's spectral packs.

## Lessons from Hearthstone Battlegrounds (deeper dive)

**What it is:** The 8-player auto-battler king. 60+ heroes, hundreds of minions, tribal synergies (Murloc, Demon, Beast, Mech, Dragon, Pirate, Quilboar, Naga, Undead, Elemental). The blueprint Pileup's lobby format borrows.

**Mechanics already covered, plus:**

- **Tribes.** Every minion has a tribe. Many minions buff their tribe. Tribes are toggled in/out each season for variety. **This is the same idea as Oaken Tower's tags.**
- **Tavern tier upgrades cost gold.** As do most actions. **Single currency** (gold). Argues against Pileup's planned two-currency system. Worth re-considering — is mana actually needed?
- **Hero powers as the run identity.** Brann Bronzebeard's HP triggers Battlecries twice. Curator starts with a tribeless 1/1. Patchwerk has 50% extra HP. Each is *the entire build's foundation*.
- **Boss positions.** Eliminated players spectate. Streamers love this — perfect content.
- **Rotating seasons.** Tribes and minions cycle every 3-4 months. Game stays fresh; meta resets.

**Ideas this sparks for Pileup:**

- **Reconsider currency split.** Hearthstone Battlegrounds works with one currency (gold). The two-currency split adds depth but also complexity. Worth re-asking: is the gain worth the cost? *Possible alternative:* one currency (gold), but **mana is earned at fixed amounts per battle** and only spent on permanent buffs (no choice involved in earning, only spending). That preserves the gold/mana feel while halving cognitive load.
- **Seasonal pack rotation.** Like Battlegrounds' tribe rotation. Every 3 months, swap which packs/tags are available in shops. Keeps the meta fresh, gives content updates a reason to exist, mitigates Oaken Tower's "only 3 viable builds" stagnation.
- **Spectator mode for eliminated players.** Watch the remaining battles. Cheer for the player who took you out. Highly streamable.
- **Captain power as run identity.** Already mentioned for Bazaar. Hearthstone confirms: this is *the* mechanic that makes a PvP auto-battler feel different each game. Pileup needs this.

## A unified pattern across ALL these games

Look at what shows up in every successful PvP auto-battler:

1. **Async PvP via ghost snapshots.** SAP, Bazaar, Oaken Tower, Backpack Battles — all use this. **Locked.**
2. **Hero/captain/character with unique pool or power.** Battlegrounds heroes, Bazaar heroes, Backpack classes, Oaken Tower starting items. **Strongly recommended for Pileup.**
3. **Tags/tribes/packs for synergy lanes.** Battlegrounds tribes, Oaken tags, Bazaar item types, Backpack adjacency. **Pileup needs this — currently only has values.**
4. **Tier system in shop (gold-cost progression).** Battlegrounds tavern tiers, Bazaar item rarities, SAP tier system, Oaken Tower star upgrades. **In design doc, locked.**
5. **Sudden-death / fatigue mechanic for stall prevention.** Bazaar sandstorm, Backpack fatigue. **Add to design doc.**
6. **Tripling / fusion for overflow value.** Battlegrounds golden, SAP leveling, Oaken stars, Bazaar rarity fusion. **Recommended for Pileup.**
7. **Pre-run identity choice.** Oaken's perks, Battlegrounds' hero pick, Bazaar's hero pick. **Strongly recommended.**

**Observation:** Pileup's design currently misses #2, #3, #5, and #7. Adding even some of these would dramatically increase staying power without changing the core "War + auto-battler" feel.

## Run-pacing redesign suggestion

Combining Bazaar's day/hour structure + Luck Be a Landlord's rent battles + Battlegrounds' lobby rhythm, here's a richer 10-battle run for Pileup:

| Battle | Type | Notes |
|--------|------|-------|
| 1 | PvP / async | Tutorial-friendly, all players have starter pile |
| 2 | PvP / async | Standard |
| 3 | **Rent (PvE)** | Beat a PvE pile by margin → bonus reward |
| 4 | PvP / async | Standard |
| 5 | **Special shop** | Triple shop slots, Joker offered |
| 6 | PvP / async | Standard |
| 7 | **Decision event** | Heal HP / take a buff / take a risk |
| 8 | PvP / async | Standard |
| 9 | **Boss (PvE)** | Beat a Boss pile with unique mechanic |
| 10 | **Final PvP** | Last opponent standing in lobby — winner takes all |

This gives the run *texture and pacing* — not a flat shop-battle-shop-battle grind. Closer to Bazaar/Slay the Spire feel.

## Action items for the design doc

Based on this Volume 2 research, the design doc should be updated with:

1. **Sandstorm/fatigue mechanic** — explicit rule for what happens if a battle is tied at flip 5.
2. **Captain/Hero system** (open question) — should each lobby slot be a different "Captain" with a unique passive?
3. **Tag/tribe system** (open question) — should cards have tags beyond their value?
4. **Pre-run Joker pick** (open question) — should players choose Jokers before the run, or only in-shop?
5. **Run-pacing texture** — should runs include PvE encounters and decision events between PvP battles?
6. **Triple/star fusion mechanic** — should buying duplicates fuse them?
7. **Rent / score-target battles** — should some battles have minimum thresholds?

Each of these is a real fork in the road. The PvP auto-battler genre says yes to most of them, but Pileup's "instant-readability via War" identity could push back on some (more mechanics = more learning curve).

**The honest tradeoff:** Every mechanic above adds depth and retention but increases barrier to entry. Pileup's strongest USP is "anyone who's played War can pick this up in 30 seconds." Don't blow that up by importing every neighboring game's complexity. Pick 2–3 of these systems to adopt, not all 7.
